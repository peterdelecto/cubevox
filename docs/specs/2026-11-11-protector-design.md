# Protector

One printed cup of Siraya Tech TPU-GF with two jobs. In use the box stands in it and the cup
lies flat on the desk under the boot. For travel the same cup turns over, side to side, goes
down over the knobs and snaps into the seam between the lid and the dish. The boot, lid and
dish carry no feature for it and do not change (owner 2026-11-11: the boot does not grow).
Supersedes `2026-11-07-protector-design.md` whole.

Heights are given from the desk up, with the boot flat on the desk and the lid face sloping
2 degrees toward Adam. "Above the plate" means measured square to the plate from its inner
face, which is the face the boot's underside lies on.

## Adam's walk

1. In use. The box stands in the cup on the desk. He sees a low tray round the boot whose rim
   stops 13.6 mm above the boot's top edge. The lid and the top of the dish stand clear above
   it. The rear wall is cut away round each jack, the power switch and the USB-C, so all of
   them are open. He plays the knobs and toggles as before.
2. Gig over. He unplugs the leads, holds the cup's side walls and pulls the box up. The boot's
   top edge meets the sloped underside of the two snap ribs, pushes the side walls out 2.25 mm
   and passes. The pull is about 28 N.
   Goes wrong: he lifts the box by the lid only. The cup comes up with it until he holds the
   cup down.
   Goes wrong: he pulls one end only. The box tilts out; the jacks leave through their
   notches, which are open at the rim. Nothing binds.
3. He turns the cup over, side to side, so its notched wall is still at the back where the
   jacks are, and lowers it over the knobs. The ribs meet the lid's top chamfer and ride down
   the lid's side walls, pressed out 0.25 mm. He pushes until the knob tops seat in the 18
   pockets inside the plate (owner 2026-11-11: the plate may rest or be pressed on the knobs;
   tolerated, not wanted). At that depth the ribs drop into the seam and he feels a click.
   Goes wrong: back to front. The closed front wall meets the jacks and stops on them. He
   turns it round.
   Goes wrong: the knob tops land beside the pockets. Each pocket has a 0.8 mm lead-in, so a
   small shift seats them.
   Goes wrong: a knob stands higher than 20 mm. The cup stops short and the ribs rest on the
   lid's wall just above the seam, held by squeeze only.
4. In the bag. The knobs and toggles are under the 3 mm plate, the lid is covered past the
   seam, and the dish shows as a band between the rim and the boot. The rear wall closes the
   space behind the knobs except at its notches. The ribs in the seam hold the cup on.
   Goes wrong: something heavy lands on the bag. The plate presses on 18 knob tops at once.
5. Next gig. He grips the cup's walls and pulls it up off the lid; the ribs click out of the
   seam. He turns it over, sets it on the desk and pushes the box down into it. The boot's
   bottom edge meets the sloped tops of the ribs and passes with about 28 N.

## Geometry

1. Plate. 3.0 mm thick, flat on both faces. In use it lies on the desk and the boot's
   underside lies on its inner face. For travel it rests on the knob tops, parallel to the
   lid face.
2. Walls. 2.0 mm thick, square to the plate, on all four sides. Inner faces 0.4 mm outside the
   boot: 196.8 mm wide, 156.9 mm front to rear. Corner radius 8.9 mm inside, 10.9 mm outside,
   concentric with the boot's corners.
3. Depth. 35.6 mm inside, 38.6 mm outside, rim level (owner 2026-11-11: 38.6 is fine). In use
   the rim is 13.6 mm above the boot's top edge and 7.3 mm (front) to 12.75 mm (rear) under
   the lid face. For travel the rim is at z -3.33, 3.5 mm past the seam's centre.
4. Rear wall (owner 2026-11-11: formed round the space the jacks need, close to the DC jack
   and the switch, no sharp corners). Notches open at the rim, given as x across the box and
   floor height above the plate:
   1. 1/4" jack: x 7.6 to 29.6, floor 17 mm. Wide enough for a plug body 20 mm across.
   2. USB-C: x 47.5 to 70.5, floor 31.6 mm. The rim would otherwise stand 0.4 mm over the
      bottom of the lid's USB-C opening and in the way of the plug's body.
   3. Combo jack: x 102 to 134.5, down to a lip 1.5 mm tall.
   4. Switch: x 134.5 to 159, floor 24.5 mm, 2.0 mm under the dish's opening.
   5. Barrel jack: x 159 to 174.6, floor 20.1 mm, 2.0 mm under and beside the dish's opening.
      A plug up to 15 mm across fits.
   Notches 3 to 5 are one opening with a stepped floor. Inside corners are rounded 4 mm and
   outside corners 3 mm; the USB-C notch and the step between the switch and barrel floors
   are rounded 2 mm.
5. Snap ribs. One on each side wall, at mid-length (centred 78.45 mm from the inner front
   face), mirrored left to right. Each is a ridge 40 mm long at its base and 5.7 mm tall at
   its base, with all four flanks at 45 degrees. Its crest stands 2.65 mm from the wall,
   32.07 mm above the plate, and is rounded 0.4 mm. The lid and dish walls are 2.4 mm from the
   cup wall, so the crest reaches 0.25 mm into the seam. The rib's top flank meets the wall
   0.68 mm under the rim.
6. Knob pockets. 18 round pockets inside the plate, 16.0 mm across and 1.5 mm deep, each with
   a 0.8 mm lead-in chamfer, one over each knob and over MENU, placed for the cup turned over.
   For travel the knob tops seat in them and set the cup's place and its depth. In use the
   boot's underside lies flat over them. 1.5 mm of plate is left under each pocket.
7. Plate edge. The outer edge of the plate carries the lid's own chamfer, 1.25 mm wide and
   2.17 mm tall.
8. Rim. 0.5 mm round, inside and outside, carried round every rear notch.
9. Inside corner. 1.5 mm fillet where the walls meet the plate.
10. Outer size. 200.8 x 160.9 x 38.6 mm. Fits the 254 mm bed flat. 136.3 cm³, 180 g.

## What holds it

1. In use. The boot sits under the two ribs, which are 7.2 mm above its top edge. The box
   leaves only when the side walls are pushed out 2.25 mm. The ribs press 0.25 mm on the dish
   and lid walls where they are not over the seam.
2. Travel. Each rib's crest sits in the seam with the wall relaxed, so the hold does not fade
   as the TPU relaxes. The knob tops in their pockets set the depth that puts the crest on
   the seam's centre.
3. Why the ribs are on the solid wall. A cover that clicks onto a box carries its snap on the
   wall and lets the wall flex. At mid-length the 2.0 mm side wall is as soft as a slotted
   finger and strains about 0.7 % when the boot passes, so no slots are needed.
4. Forces, estimates from the data sheet's 450 MPa and the wall taken as a strip fixed at the
   plate. The wall gives about 2.65 N per mm at a rib. The boot pushes each wall out 2.25 mm,
   about 6 N a side, so the box takes about 28 N to push in or pull out. Leaving the seam
   takes 0.25 mm, so the hold for travel is about 4 to 7 N, two to four times the cup's
   weight. The walls bend across the layers, where the data sheet gives no figure.
5. The snap is 0.25 mm, about one layer. The first print's hold could be off by a factor of
   two either way. `snapStand` tunes it.

## Print

1. Siraya Tech TPU-GF, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers.
2. Plate down on a textured sheet, walls up. The textured face is the travel top.
3. No supports. The face on the bed is flat. Each rib's underside is a 45 degree slope.
4. Plate, walls and ribs are explicit solids. No infill volume.
5. Fit is built for the first print: 0.4 mm air at the boot covers swell. No feature is under
   0.4 mm except the snap's 0.25 mm reach into the seam.

## Decisions

1. No change to the boot, lid or dish (owner 2026-11-11). Windows in the boot cannot be
   reached: a cup this shallow ends above the boot when it is over the knobs.
2. Plate flat on the desk in use; its slope for travel does not matter (owner 2026-11-11).
3. Nothing covers the jacks or switches in use (owner 2026-11-11). First built with the whole
   rear open; the owner then asked for a rear wall formed round the space the jacks need.
4. Height. The owner chose a low cup ("35 seems fine"), then 36.5 mm, then 38.6 mm so the
   ribs have room for a 45 degree lead under the rim (2026-11-11).
5. The plate rests on the knobs for travel (owner 2026-11-11). No stop on the box.
6. The hold is a snap into the seam (owner 2026-11-11: the cup can grab it). An earlier line
   here said the seam was too shallow to catch; that was wrong.
7. No squeeze pads and no fingers. Pads that gripped the lid by friction had to be pushed out
   3.0 mm by the boot, because the boot is 2.0 mm wider per side than the lid. Built on
   slotted fingers they gave about 3.5 N of hold for about 60 N of push and pull, stayed bent
   in both positions, and put eight slots in the side walls. The owner dropped them
   (2026-11-11). A snap gives more hold for less push because it holds by its shape.
8. No underside relief (owner 2026-11-11). That face lies on the bed, so a recess in it has
   nothing under it and cannot print cleanly. A flush two-colour inlay in that face is still
   open; it is the owner's taste.
9. Other approaches were weighed on 2026-11-11. A cover fitted to the lid that the box stands
   on in use (proof P3) was set aside: the protector is stashed nested, with the box in it.
   A second loose part is out (owner). A stepped cup and a tall cup that reaches the boot
   were set aside for their height.
